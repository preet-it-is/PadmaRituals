A short history of tea

Two legends, one leaf.

Long before chai had spices and cups had saucers, tea had stories. China tells two of them best: one about an emperor, one about a monk.

Legend  ·  around 2737 BCE

Shennong and the falling leaf

The tale says Emperor Shennong, the divine farmer who tested herbs to learn their powers, was boiling drinking water when a few leaves from a wild tea tree drifted into the pot. The water turned a pale gold. He tasted it, found it fragrant and refreshing, and tea was born.

Legend  ·  around the 5th to 6th century CE

Bodhidharma and the nine-year vigil

Bodhidharma, the monk who carried Buddhist meditation from India to China, is said to have sat facing a wall for nine years. When sleep finally overtook him, he was so angry that he cut off his eyelids. Where they fell, the first tea plants are said to have sprouted, and tea became the monks' companion for staying awake.

Both are legends, not recorded history. What they agree on is that tea began in China, grew into a ritual of calm and attention, and then travelled along trade routes across Asia until it reached India and became chai.
